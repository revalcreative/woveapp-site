Why Fleece Is a Common Source

Fleece fabric starts as a polyester knit. After knitting, it’s brushed on one or both sides to raise the fiber loops and create the characteristic soft, fluffy texture. This process — called “napping” or “raising” — dramatically increases the surface area of exposed fiber ends.

Those exposed loops and ends are what make fleece so soft and insulating. They also make it one of the highest-shedding fabric types: there are enormously more loose fiber ends per square centimeter than in a smooth woven or flat-knit polyester.

Early research on microplastic shedding found that a single fleece jacket washing could release over 1 million fibers. Subsequent research has produced a range of estimates depending on jacket construction, weight, age, and wash conditions — but fleece consistently ranks among the highest shedders of any commonly worn fabric.

The environmental concern is significant: polyester fibers don’t biodegrade. They persist in the environment indefinitely, accumulating in waterways, soil, food chains, and increasingly in human tissue.

What Happens in the Wash

When a fleece jacket goes into a washing machine:

  1. Water and mechanical agitation work on the brushed surface
  2. The loosely held fiber ends and loops are dislodged
  3. Hundreds of thousands to millions of fibers enter the wash water
  4. Standard machine drain filters are too coarse to catch them
  5. Fibers pass to the wastewater system

The first few washes of a new fleece shed the most — that initial loose surface fiber population is the highest it will ever be. Subsequent washes continue to shed but at lower rates as the more easily dislodged fibers have already been released.

How to Reduce Shedding

Use a Guppyfriend bag: This is the most effective consumer tool for fleece specifically. Put the jacket inside the bag before washing. Research found it captures a significant proportion of shed fibers. It also appears to reduce the physical agitation the jacket undergoes inside the bag, further reducing total shedding.

Wash cold, gentle cycle: Hot water and aggressive mechanical agitation both increase fiber release. Cold water + delicate cycle is meaningfully lower-shed than hot water + heavy cycle.

Wash less frequently: Fleece jackets rarely need washing after every wear. Airing them out and spot-cleaning small stains extends the time between full washes. Many people wash fleece once every 5–10 wears.

Install a drain filter: External washing machine filters (PlanetCare, Lint LUV-R) installed at the drain outlet catch fibers from all laundry including fleece that sheds despite being in a Guppyfriend bag.

Dry with low heat or air dry: Dryer heat degrades synthetic fibers faster, increasing future shedding. Air drying or low-heat tumble drying extends the fabric’s lifespan and reduces per-wash shed rates over time.

Safer Alternatives to Consider

If you’re shopping for a new midlayer or cozy jacket, these alternatives offer insulation without fleece’s shedding profile:

Wool (boiled wool or knit wool): Natural fiber, much lower shedding than fleece, and sheds protein fibers rather than plastic. Boiled wool is dense and sturdy; knit wool is lighter.

Down or synthetic insulation jackets: Puffer-style insulation doesn’t have a fleece outer — the outer shell is typically a woven fabric, lower-shed than a brushed polyester surface. (Note: down is a higher-PFAS-risk category for the shell’s DWR treatment — look for PFAS-free certification.)

Merino wool midlayers: Merino sweaters and midlayers provide warmth without fleece’s shedding characteristics. High cost, high performance.

Corduroy or velvet (cotton or wool-based): These have a pile surface but in natural fibers — lower microplastic concern than synthetic fleece.

FAQ

Is all fleece high-shedding?
Most synthetic fleece is high-shedding. Natural fiber “fleece” (like a boiled wool or heavy wool knit) doesn’t have the same shedding profile — it sheds natural fibers, not plastic.

Does fleece shed when I’m wearing it (not just washing)?
Yes, but at lower rates than during washing. Physical activity, sitting on upholstery, and friction during movement all cause some fiber release. Washing is the primary event.

Is recycled fleece better for shedding?
Recycled fleece (made from recycled polyester bottles or garments) has the same shedding behavior as virgin fleece. The environmental benefit is in sourcing, not washing performance.

Should I throw out my fleece jacket?
The most sustainable choice is to continue using what you own. Use a Guppyfriend bag, wash less often, and wash cold. When it’s genuinely worn out, consider natural fiber alternatives.
